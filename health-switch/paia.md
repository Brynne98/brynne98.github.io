# PAIA Manual

_Prepared under section 51 of the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2
of 2000 (PAIA), following the Information Regulator's template for private
bodies. Last updated: 17 October 2026._

## 1. About this manual

This manual is for Brynne Prince, a sole proprietor in South Africa who
publishes the **Health Switch RSA** app for iPhone and Android. It says
what records we hold, how to ask for them, and how we process personal
information under the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013
(POPIA).

## 7. Processing of personal information

### 7.1 Purpose

To run the App and its shared directory, to process and restore purchases,
to fix crashes and keep the App fast, to show ads to phones without Pro,
and to answer people who contact us. The [Privacy Policy](privacy.html)
gives the detail for each.

### 7.2 Data subjects and their information

| Data subject | Information |
|---|---|
| People who use the App | Anonymous installation identities, contributions, reports, feedback, purchase history (product, date, price), device model and system version, usage events, crash reports, and, through AdMob, an advertising identifier where allowed and an approximate location from the IP address. |
| People named in the directory | Work names or roles, and the hospital line they answer. |
| People who email us | Name, email address and what they wrote. |

The App never asks for names, email addresses, phone numbers,
professional registration or patient information.

### 7.3 Recipients

The services that process data for us, each for the purpose given in the
Privacy Policy: Convex (server), Expo (updates and launch speed), PostHog
(analytics), Sentry (crash reports), RevenueCat (purchases), Google AdMob
(ads), and Apple and Google (app stores and payment). Contributions are
shown to every user of the App. We do not sell personal information.

### 7.4 Transfers outside South Africa

Convex and Expo are in the United States. PostHog and Sentry use EU
servers. Google, Apple and RevenueCat may process data in the United
States and other countries. These transfers follow section 72 of POPIA, as
set out in the Privacy Policy.

### 7.5 Security

Data travels encrypted (HTTPS) between the App and each service. The
anonymous identity is kept in the phone's secure storage. Only the
publisher has admin access to the server and the services' dashboards.

## 8. How to ask for a record

1. Fill in **Form 2** (Request for Access to Record), from the
   Regulator:
   https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/InfoRegSA-PAIA-Form02-Reg7.pdf
2. Email it to brynneprince98@gmail.com with proof of identity. If you
   ask for someone else, add proof that you may act for them.
3. Say which records you want, the form of access you want (copy, email,
   inspection), and how we should reply.
4. For a record that is not your own personal information, say which
   right you want to use or protect, and why you need the record for it
   (PAIA section 53).

If you cannot fill in the form because of illiteracy or a disability, you
may make the request by email or phone after contacting us, and we will
help.

A request for your own personal information, or to correct or delete it,
can also be made under POPIA by a plain email. See "Your rights" in the
[Privacy Policy](privacy.html).

## 9. Fees

PAIA allows a request fee and an access fee, as set in the PAIA
Regulations. We charge no request fee. If a request needs copies or time
beyond what the Regulations allow free of charge, we will tell you the fee
before we do the work. There is never a fee to ask for your own personal
information.

## 10. Decision and refusal

We decide within 30 days of receiving the request and tell you the
result, with reasons if we refuse. We may extend this once, by up to 30
days, for a large or complex request, and will tell you in writing.

We may refuse access on the grounds in Chapter 4 of Part 3 of PAIA,
mainly to protect:

- the privacy of another person;
- another party's commercial or confidential information;
- records privileged from production in legal proceedings;
- our own commercial information, including the App's source code.

We also refuse requests that are clearly frivolous or vexatious, or that
would take an unreasonable diversion of resources.

If you disagree with a decision, you may complain to the Information
Regulator within 180 days, or apply to court.
